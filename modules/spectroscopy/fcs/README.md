# `spectroscopy/fcs` — Fluorescence Correlation Spectroscopy (FCS)

Software correlator algorithms for computing auto- and cross-correlation functions from photon streams.

## Contents

- **`Correlator.h` / `Correlator.cpp`**: Multi-tau software correlator for non-uniformly spaced photon arrival times.
- **`CorrelatorCurve.h` / `CorrelatorCurve.cpp`**: Container and manipulation functions for correlation curves.
- **`CorrelatorPhotonStream.h` / `CorrelatorPhotonStream.cpp`**: Efficient photon stream indexing for correlation calculations.
- **`Fdc2D.h` / `Fdc2D.cpp`**: The 2D fluorescence-decay correlation (2D-FLC) photon pass — `fdc_scan_log` builds one log-binned photon-pair matrix per lag in a single traversal, with `fdc_log` for the single-lag case and `fdc_log_ticks` / `fdc_log_bin` exposing the axis it counts on. Correlation over *micro*-times rather than intensity: `M[a][b]` counts pairs separated by roughly `dT` whose two photons landed in micro-time bins `a` and `b`, so off-diagonal weight means the emitter changed its decay in between and the lag dependence measures the interconversion rate. The inversions that turn the matrix into lifetimes and rates are the caller's. Two contracts worth knowing before calling: the result is exactly independent of `n_chunks` (private `int64` accumulators, summed), and a micro-time outside the window is dropped rather than clamped into the edge bin.

  The method is 2D-FLC spectroscopy (Ishii & Tahara, "Two-Dimensional Fluorescence Lifetime Correlation Spectroscopy. 1. Principle" and ". 2. Application", J. Phys. Chem. B 117(39), 11414–11422 and 11423–11432, 2013, doi:10.1021/jp406861u, doi:10.1021/jp406864e; single-molecule application: Kondo et al., Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 116(23), 11247–11252, 2019, doi:10.1073/pnas.1821207116). The kernels reproduce the original author's `TK_Create2DFDC_04.m` (T. Kondo, Schlau-Cohen lab, MIT) exactly, including its log-axis quantization — the reference keeps its edges real-valued and compares the integer tick against them, so the effective integer edge is the *floor*, and `test_fdc2d.py::TestAgainstTheOriginalMatlab` pins the library against the .m's own output (run in Octave, recorded in `test/data/reference/fdc2d_matlab_tk_create2dfdc04.npz`).

  **How good is the dynamics?** Benchmarked on simulated experiments with known ground truth (`examples/correlation/plot_fdc_2d_dynamics_resolution.py`, seed-replicated): with a 10 ms macro window and 80 ms lag window, a two-state emitter's relaxation is recovered within 25% everywhere from **50 ms to 10 s** — and nowhere below the lag-window width (80 ms), which is the resolution floor of the *method*, not of the pass. A three-state chain with two relaxation times (1 s and 10 s) is resolved on the seed-averaged curve (fitted 0.86 and 7.9 s; a single exponential fits neither mode at 4.96 s); per-stream fits recover the fast mode but the slow mode needs averaging or a longer stream at ~300k photons. The introductory walkthrough is `examples/correlation/plot_fdc_2d.py`.

  **And microsecond dynamics — single molecule, T3 mode** (`examples/correlation/plot_fdc_2d_microsecond_fret.py`): with the macro clock at the laser period (25 ns, 40 MHz), one immobilized FRET molecule's donor channel at 500 kcps, states E = 0.2/0.8 (τ 3.2/0.8 ns), and a 75 ns-effective lag window, relaxations from **200 ns to 10 µs are all recovered within 25%** (3 seeds each; consistent −15…−22% bias belonging to the deliberately simple log-linear estimator; seed spread ±3%). The floor again tracks the window: 200 ns sits 2.7× above the 75 ns span. Caveats kept honest in the example: Poisson emission only (no afterpulsing, dead time, or dark counts — real µs-lag data fights exactly those), and an immobilized continuous stream rather than aggregated bursts.

  **The same, with freely diffusing molecules** (`examples/correlation/plot_fdc_2d_microsecond_fret_diffusion.py`): open-volume diffusion at τ_diff = 2 ms (D = 11.25 µm²/s, w0 = 0.3 µm), ~40 injected molecules (~0.15 in focus, 254 kcps). Microsecond recovery survives the dilution — 200 ns…10 µs fitted within 0.7–27% (wider seed scatter than immobilized; the 5 µs point marginal) — and the **diffusion time is the ceiling** exactly as the window width is the floor: a 5 ms relaxation is gated away (each seed fits ~τ_diff, not the kinetics). Two findings the diluted regime forced, recorded in the example: the total-variation statistic carries a √(K/4N) shot-noise pedestal (~0.018 at 36k pairs — flat in single-lifetime and shuffled controls, invisible in the immobilized case whose signal was 10× taller), so the pair micro-time **covariance** — computed from the same `fdc_scan_log` matrix, per-pair noise, no positive bias — is the right statistic there (TV fit: 0.52 µs against 1.0 true; covariance: 1.04 µs); and the engine window can be coarser than the T3 clock (2.5 µs windows, ticks reconstructed as `(window·dt + arrival)/25 ns`) with nothing load-bearing lost — 5 s streams in ~9 s of wall time instead of minutes.
